HIPAA design note

This tool runs entirely in your browser. It does not sit in a HIPAA-compliant environment by itself. To stay compliant, it never asks for and should never receive Protected Health Information.

When you add a patient, the tool generates a random private identifier (example: PT-X7K2-9M). Write that token next to the real patient record in your HIPAA-compliant EHR or chart. Inside this tool, you only see the token. The mapping between token and real patient lives only in your HIPAA environment, never here. Do not enter names, MRNs, dates of birth, addresses, room numbers tied to a known unit, or any other identifier. Visit notes follow the same rule: no PHI.

    Evidence base

    Every weighting and threshold in this tool maps to a validated instrument or published benchmark. Citations below.

    Acuity instruments

    • HOPE (Hospice Outcomes & Patient Evaluation): CMS-mandated assessment effective October 1, 2025. Replaces HIS. Captures 8 symptom-impact items (pain, dyspnea, anxiety, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, agitation) on a 0–3 scale at admission and HOPE Update Visits (HUVs) days 6–15 and 16–30. Any moderate or severe symptom triggers a Symptom Follow-up Visit within 2 calendar days. CMS HOPE
    • Palliative Performance Scale (PPS): Validated 11-point scale (0%–100%) developed by Victoria Hospice Society. Strong correlation with survival (r ≈ 0.77 with PPSv2). PPS ≤ 50% is hospice-eligible for most non-cancer diagnoses; ≤ 70% for oncology. Originally designed for "analysis of home nursing care workload." ePrognosis
    • IPOS (Integrated Palliative care Outcome Scale): 17-item validated multidimensional scale. Internal consistency α = 0.77. Higher scores in patients with unstable or deteriorating Phase of Illness vs stable. Sterie 2019
    • ESAS-r (Edmonton Symptom Assessment System Revised): 10-item patient-reported, 0–10 intensity. Daily completion recommended in hospice and tertiary palliative units. AHS manual
    • Phase of Illness: 5-phase clinical state (Stable, Unstable, Deteriorating, Dying, Deceased) developed for AN-SNAP palliative casemix in Australia. Inter-rater reliability validated (Masso 2015). Drives case-mix funding and is the strongest single non-functional predictor of care needs. PoI cross-sectional
    • AN-SNAP v5 (Australian National Subacute and Non-Acute Patient Classification): Combines care type, Phase of Illness, functional impairment, and age into casemix groups. The international reference for palliative-care workload classification. IHACPA

    Staffing standards

    • NHPCO Hospice Staffing Framework (March 2024): Declines to set a single caseload number. Recommends individualized adjustment using organizational variables and outcome measures. NHPCO PDF
    • Home hospice RN case manager caseload: Industry benchmark 12–14 patients per RN case manager (Multi-View Benchmarking). Default in this tool: 13. Industry reality: 16–24 not uncommon (associated with burnout and quality drift). Multi-View
    • Inpatient hospice RN ratio: Federal minimum is one nurse per 8 patients with at least 2 direct-care staff per shift and at least one RN providing direct care when any GIP patient is present. IAHPC recommended optimum: 1 RN per 1.5–1.7 patients over 24 hours. Default in this tool: 1:1.6. IAHPC
    • 42 CFR § 418.64 (Conditions of Participation, Core Services): Hospice must provide nursing, medical social services, counseling, and physician services routinely and substantially through hospice employees. Contracted staff allowed for surge and short-term gaps. eCFR 418.64
    • Continuous Home Care (CHC): Minimum 8 hours direct care in 24h, of which > 50% must be nursing (RN/LVN). 4 hours nursing + 4 hours aide is the floor. PCNOW FF #507
    • Service Intensity Add-On (SIA): Last 7 days of life, in 15-minute units up to 4 hours per day, paid at the CHC hourly rate for RN/MSW visits. Cumulative mean increase of 15.7 minutes per beneficiary in the last 7 days post-implementation. Cagle 2017

    Visit-time defaults seeded into the tool

    • RHC RN routine visit average 45–60 minutes (CMS family experience benchmark: "4–5 visits per week, about an hour each"). Tier-weighted in this tool from 45 (T1) to 75 (T5). Medbridge
    • RN admission visit: 60–120 minutes typical. Default 90.
    • Death visit / pronouncement: 60–90 minutes. Default 90.
    • Recert visit: +30 minutes added to routine visit time.
    • IDG / documentation: ~10 minutes per patient per week of indirect time (built into productivity factor).
    • Travel: 1.3–1.5x multiplier on direct minutes for home hospice (default 1.4). Urban programs trend lower, rural higher.

    Acuity numbering system (Tier I to V)

    • Tier I (Stable): PPS 60 to 100, Phase Stable, all symptoms 0 or 1. Routine visit cadence 1 per week.
    • Tier II (Watchful): PPS 50 to 60, Stable to Deteriorating, mild to moderate symptom. 2 visits per week.
    • Tier III (Active): PPS 40, Deteriorating, moderate symptom impact. Triggers HOPE Symptom Follow-up. 3 visits per week.
    • Tier IV (Crisis-risk): PPS 20 to 30, Unstable phase, or any severe symptom. 4 to 5 visits per week. Consider CHC.
    • Tier V (Dying / Maximum support): PPS at or below 20, Dying phase, or on GIP. Daily visits, SIA window applies, 2-person tasks common.

    HIPAA-compliant design

    • This tool stores data locally on the device only. It is not, by itself, a HIPAA-compliant environment. Compliance is achieved by keeping all Protected Health Information out of the tool.
    • The tool generates a random opaque private identifier (e.g., PT-X7K2-9M) for each patient. The mapping between that token and the real patient must live only in your HIPAA-compliant EHR or chart. Inside this tool, only the token is visible.
    • Drive zones must be regional tags or 3-digit ZIP prefixes. Per HIPAA Safe Harbor (45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)), the first 3 digits of a ZIP code are de-identified only when the combined population of that ZIP3 region exceeds 20,000. Full 5-digit ZIPs and street addresses are PHI and must not be entered.
    • Staff names are not PHI for patient privacy purposes and can be entered as displayed (e.g., "Maria S., RN").
    • Export files (JSON, CSV) contain whatever is in this tool. They are still safe to back up to a HIPAA-compliant location and unsafe to share with anyone who lacks the mapping file. Treat them as deidentified per HIPAA Safe Harbor when only tokens and clinical data are present.

    Composite acuity formula

    Acuity = 0.20 × PPS_inverted + 0.30 × Symptom_impact + 0.20 × Phase + 0.20 × Level_of_care + 0.10 × Psychosocial. Tier = ceil(score / 20), bounded 1–5. Weights are editable in the tool's settings once you have enough logged data to justify a change.

    This tool supports clinical judgment. It does not replace it. Acuity scoring is a starting point for case conferences, not a substitute for a clinician assessing a patient.
